# Phase 2 plan — the content bridge (SRD-only modules)

Implementation plan for Phase 2 of [the osrlib-referee spec](spec.md). Phase 0 proved the wire; Phase 1 proved the **thesis** — that moving all mechanics and state into the `osrlib` engine behind a small tool surface makes a real B/X delve playable while collapsing the mechanical/state token overhead — on one **native**, Python-authored adventure. Phase 2 confronts what Phase 1 deferred: **turning a published (or published-shaped) adventure module into playable content**, paying the transcription tax with eyes open.

**Scope decision (this plan): Phase 2 is scoped to content the OSE SRD can express.** The spec's original Phase 2 led with an injectable-catalog engine change (its "2a") ahead of the bundle format and compiler. This plan **drops that engine change** and its entire catalog-injection surface, and defers custom/unique stat blocks to a future phase (see "Why SRD-only, and what it defers"). What remains is the useful, shippable core — the bundle format, the loader, and the compiler — all built in *this* repo against the stock SRD catalog, with **zero engine change and no behavioral skill change**. The spec was amended (2026-07-06) to match this scope-down: its roadmap, risks, and pinned/resolved decisions now describe the SRD-only Phase 2 (see spec §Resolved decisions).

Every engine claim below is grounded in verified v1.1.0 source (`~/repos/osrlib-python`, tag `v1.1.0`, HEAD `30d6c31`), read against the installed source during planning.

## The milestone

One small **openly-licensed or original-authored** module (never a commercial one — see Licensing) is **compiled into an adventure bundle and played end-to-end** through the existing MCP tool surface, with a documented log of everything the compile approximated — **including every non-SRD creature or item reskinned to its nearest SRD template** — or pushed to the authorial escape hatch. "Played end-to-end" is Phase 1's loop — `session_new` on the bundle, `observe`/`execute`/`prose` through a delve, durable save — but now the content is a *loaded, compiled bundle*, not a native Python builder. One milestone, no staging, no upstream dependency.

## Why SRD-only, and what it defers

Planning verified *why* unique content is genuinely hard, and that finding is exactly what makes SRD-only a clean boundary rather than a cop-out.

**The injection seam does not exist, and the affordance that looks like it is a trap.** `validate_adventure(adventure, monsters, equipment)` (`crawl/adventure.py:94`) takes the catalogs as parameters, so a module's unique `template_id` *passes* a standalone `validate_adventure(adv, merged, merged)`. But every **runtime** resolution hardcodes the process-global `@cache`d SRD singletons: `GameSession.new` calls `validate_adventure(adventure, load_monsters(), load_equipment())` against the stock catalogs (`crawl/session.py:273`), and spawn/take/equip read the globals directly (`session.py:417`, `exploration.py:852`, `session.py:764/813`, the core layer). Those loaders are bound **by name at import** (`from osrlib.data import load_monsters, load_equipment` at `session.py:85`, `exploration.py:129`) and `@cache`d, so even a monkeypatch wouldn't reach them. A unique creature therefore **validates and then throws `ValueError` inside `GameSession.new`, and again at spawn.** Supporting true customs would require making the catalogs session-owned and threading them through every resolution site (across `crawl` *and* the `core` layer, which cannot import `crawl`) — a real cross-cutting upstream `osrlib` change (the spec's 2a). That is deliberately **not** taken on now.

**The reskin is complete: a non-SRD creature becomes its nearest SRD template — stats and mechanical identity both.** A module's "barrow wight" compiles to `KeyedMonster(template_id="skeleton")` and *is* an SRD skeleton to the engine. There is no attempt to preserve the source identity mechanically: the engine surfaces a monster's name in player-visible events (`EncounterStartedEvent.monster_name`, `Visibility.PLAYER`, `events.py:306`; `MonsterDefeatedEvent` carries the raw `template_id`, not a display name, `events.py:454`) and `KeyedMonster` has no field to override it (`dungeon.py:394-418`), so rather than build a fragile prose-substitution layer to rename the creature everywhere the engine names it, the reskin accepts the SRD identity outright. This costs nothing in narration: by constitution Article II.3 the referee already describes a monster by *appearance*, not by announcing its type-name ("a skeletal guardian rises from the sarcophagus," never "a skeleton (AC 7, HD 1)"), so the reskin need never surface either name in the fiction — the substitution lives only in the engine's events and the manifest's `approximations` audit (module "barrow wight" → SRD `skeleton`). So the reskin needs **no engine change** (a reskinned `template_id` resolves like `skeleton`/`giant_rat` already do) **and no behavioral skill change** (it is just an SRD monster the existing constitution already governs). Items follow the same rule where an SRD base exists (a "Sword of the Barrow-Kings" is an SRD `sword`).

**What SRD-only genuinely defers — stated honestly, not hidden.** A creature whose *mechanics themselves* are the encounter — a boss whose unique special attack is the whole fight, a monster no SRD template meaningfully approximates — cannot be reskinned without gutting the beat. Such content, and true custom *magic* items with novel mechanics, are **out of scope**; they go to the authorial escape hatch (a live `SpawnMonsters` of an SRD stand-in, or a `RollDice`-adjudicated bespoke effect) or wait for the deferred injectable-catalog engine change. The compiler's approximation log names every such boundary explicitly, so a reviewer sees the fidelity budget rather than discovering it in play. The reskin's own cost — a module's evocatively-named creature loses that name and plays as its plain SRD nearest-likeness — is accepted, and logged, not smoothed over.

## Scope

In scope:

- **The bundle format + loader (work item A):** the on-disk bundle (an `Adventure` spec + a prose sidecar + a manifest), the loader, and the server changes that discover and serve bundles (`session_new`/`list_adventures`/`prose` extended beyond the native registry; per-adventure save ids). The `OsrlibError` → tool-error map extended to bundle-load failures.
- **The compiler skill + helpers (work item B):** the compiler skill and the deterministic, **compilation-only** helper tools it leans on (`validate_bundle`, an edge-key computer, a map renderer for the review gate) — exposed off the play tool surface so they cost play sessions nothing. The **reskin stage** is a first-class part of the compiler.
- **The demo module:** one open/original module (with at least one reskin, to exercise that path) plus the approximation/escape-hatch log the DoD requires.
- **Tests:** a fixture-bundle round-trip and in-process play test (extending Phase 1's `test_delve_golden` approach), a `validate_bundle` test (edge-key integrity, wandering-table resolution), the registry-union discovery test, the session-scoped prose test, and the per-adventure save test.

Out of scope (named so the phase does not over-reach):

- **The injectable-catalog engine change and all custom-catalog content** — deferred. No session-owned catalogs, no `catalog.json`, no custom monster/item stat blocks. Genuinely novel creatures/items are the reskin policy or the escape hatch, per above.
- **Any prose-driven monster-renaming / name-override machinery** — explicitly rejected. A reskinned creature is just an SRD monster; there is no session-scoped name map and **no behavioral change to the `play` skill or constitution** (the constitution's existing information discipline already governs narrating any monster — Article II.3, by appearance, not by type-name). At most two `SKILL.md` doc glosses get a one-line update — the `list_adventures` description (it now returns discovered bundles too) and the save-slot default note (adventure-scoped, see work item A) — neither a behavioral change.
- **Custom spells and custom magic-item / treasure-table content** — also catalog-injection-shaped; out of scope on the same grounds. A module's custom spell or novel-mechanic magic item is the authorial escape hatch.
- **Multi-level dungeons and `TransitionSpec` (stairs/chutes)** beyond what a chosen demo module happens to need. The content model supports them; the compiler handles them only if the demo requires them.
- **The player-facing character-creation tool, town/economy commands, and level-up UI** — Phase 3. Phase 2 keeps Phase 1's pregen-party default (or an accepted `party_document`).
- **The eval/replay harness** — Phase 4. A compiled SRD-only bundle is already a seed-deterministic, replayable trajectory (no catalog to re-supply), so Phase 4 is unblocked, but no scoring harness is built here.
- **The token measurement** — Phase 1's deferred Milestone-B follow-on, not re-opened here.

## Work item A — the bundle format + loader (this repo)

`osrlib` ships **no adventure file format and no adventure loader** (confirmed: the only `stamp_document` kinds are `character`/`party`/`save`; there is no `Adventure.from_file`, no YAML/JSON adventure loader; adventures are built in pure Python today, e.g. `examples/tui_crawler/content.py`). The bundle format and loader are entirely this repo's to invent, from the building blocks `osrlib` does provide: the `Adventure` model, `model_validate`/`model_dump`, `validate_adventure`, and the `stamp_document`/`check_document` envelope helpers.

### The bundle format

A bundle is a **directory** (so private and in-repo bundles share one shape), on-disk as **JSON** (osrlib persistence is JSON-native; `Adventure.model_validate` round-trips a `model_dump(mode="json")` dict, and the frozen models with no computed/private fields round-trip completely, re-firing their validators on load so a malformed bundle raises at load rather than silently):

- `adventure.json` — `Adventure.model_dump(mode="json")`. Carries everything `validate_adventure` needs: `name`, `town`, `dungeons` (≥1), each level's `entrance`/`width`/`height`/`edges`/`areas`, keyed `template_id`s (**always stock SRD ids** — reskins have already been resolved to SRD templates at compile time), feature `item_ids`, and the nested **core** models that must round-trip transitively (`WanderingSpec.table` as an `EncounterTable`, `Coins`, `SaveSpec`, `Condition`).
- `prose.json` — the sidecar, `{area_id: {read_aloud, referee_notes}}`, keyed by `AreaSpec.id` (`dungeon.py:447`) — the only stable prose handle, since `AreaSpec.description` (`dungeon.py:449`) is one opaque, engine-ignored string with no player/referee split. The reserved key **`"town"`** (not an `AreaSpec.id`) carries town prose, mirroring Phase 1's `PROSE_SIDECAR` (`content.py:188`) and `observe`'s `area.id == "town"`. Phase 1's sidecar was a Python literal colocated in `content.py`; a compiled bundle **must** be on-disk data because a private module's bundle can never be committed as in-repo Python (Licensing). Prose sets the scene; it does **not** rename a reskinned creature (see "Why SRD-only").
- `manifest.json` — `{bundle_id, name, description, osrlib_version, license, approximations}`. `osrlib_version` records the version the bundle was compiled and validated against (provenance, and forward-cover for the day a customs-supporting bundle format arrives). `approximations` is the DoD's audit log — an object with `reskins`, `geometry`, and `escape_hatch` lists: each reskin entry is `{source_name, template_id, area_id}` (the module called it *source_name*; it is statted and named as SRD *template_id*), each geometry entry names a non-grid approximation, each escape-hatch entry names a beat the content model couldn't express. **There is no `catalog.json`** — an SRD-only bundle injects nothing.

### The loader and the server changes

- **`Adventure.model_validate(adventure_dict)` → `GameSession.new(party, adventure, seed=…)`.** `GameSession.new` self-validates against the stock catalog (`session.py:273`), so no catalog is passed and no separate `validate_adventure` call is needed — this is the whole reason an SRD-only bundle needs no engine change (it is literally Phase 1's resolution path reached from a file). Bundle-load failures map through the existing `OsrlibError` taxonomy: a malformed spec or an id that doesn't resolve against stock → `ContentValidationError` → tool error; unknown `bundle_id` → tool error (server-defined).
- **Discovery replaces the single native registry with a union.** Today `ADVENTURE_REGISTRY` is native Python builders and `prose` reads the module-global `PROSE_SIDECAR` (`server.py:140`, `content.py:187`). Phase 2 extends this so `list_adventures`/`session_new` resolve **either** a native builder **or** a discovered on-disk bundle, discovered from `adventures/` in-repo (open/original bundles) and from the game directory (private compiled bundles). `prose` becomes **session-scoped**: the [`SessionStore`][osrlib_referee_mcp.store.SessionStore] holds the active adventure's prose map (native `PROSE_SIDECAR` or the loaded bundle's `prose.json`), and `prose(area_id)` resolves against it — two adventures may share an area id, so a module-global lookup is wrong once more than one adventure exists.
- **Save ids stay globally unique — by scoping the default slot to the adventure, not by changing `session_load`.** Phase 2 makes many coexisting adventures the norm, which breaks Phase 1's shared-`"default"` assumption: `find_save` globs `*/{save_id}.json` and raises "ambiguous" when two adventures share a slot (`gamedir.py:58-62`), so two bundles each saved at `"default"` collide on `session_load("default")`. The fix keeps `session_load(save_id)`'s by-id-alone signature (`find_save` is deliberately "locate by id without knowing the adventure", `gamedir.py:41`) and touches no skill behavior: `session_new`'s default slot becomes **adventure-scoped** (e.g. the `adventure_id` itself) instead of the literal shared `"default"`, so every default save id is globally unique by construction; a player-named slot stays the player's responsibility, with the existing "ambiguous" error as the backstop. **Rejected:** adding an `adventure_id` argument to `session_load` — a skill-visible signature change (`SKILL.md` documents `session_load(save_id)`) that the no-behavioral-skill-change invariant forbids.
- **Where bundles live.** In-repo `adventures/` holds only openly-licensed or original bundles (this repo's `AGENTS.md` §Repository layout and §Licensing). Compiled bundles for non-open modules are written to the user's game directory by default and never committed.

### Tests

A committed **fixture bundle** (a tiny stock-SRD adventure, licensing-safe) round-trips: `model_validate` reconstructs it, `validate_adventure` passes, and an in-process scripted delve drives it through the real server tool functions to a save round-trip — the Phase-1 `test_delve_golden` pattern applied to a *loaded* bundle. Plus the `validate_bundle` test (below), a discovery/registry-union test, a prose-resolution test proving `prose` reads the active session's sidecar, and a per-adventure save-disambiguation test.

## Work item B — the adventure-module-compiler skill (this repo)

The compiler reads a module (PDF/MD) and emits a bundle. It is an LLM-driven **skill** (like `bx-referee`'s content work), because interpretation — reading a map, splitting boxed text from DM notes, deciding what is a keyed encounter vs a trick, **choosing the nearest SRD template for a non-SRD creature** — is judgment, not computation. But everything where **correctness is checkable** is a deterministic, **compilation-only** helper the skill runs (exposed off the play tool surface — see below), so the fidelity-critical parts don't ride on model arithmetic.

### The compile stages

- **Prose → sidecar.** Split each area's authored text into `read_aloud` (the module's boxed/read-aloud text, delivered verbatim in play) and `referee_notes` (DM notes, tactics, development), keyed by the `AreaSpec.id` the geometry stage assigns.
- **Geometry → grid + `edges` — the dominant cost and the likeliest place fidelity slips.** The `edges` map is the heavy transcription: walls are the default, so every passage and door is an explicit canonical `"{x},{y}:north|west"` entry via `edge_key` (`dungeon.py:118-137`), and a 20×20 level has hundreds of interior edges. Non-grid or organic maps are approximated to the grid, with the fidelity loss **logged** to the manifest. The skill computes edge keys through the `edge_key` helper tool, never by hand.
- **Keyed content → specs, with the reskin stage.** Encounters → `KeyedEncounter`/`KeyedMonster`. A creature already in the SRD resolves to its stock `template_id`. A **non-SRD creature is reskinned**: the skill picks the nearest SRD template by mechanical profile (HD, AC, attacks/damage, movement, morale, special qualities), emits *that* stock `template_id`, and records `{source_name, template_id, area_id}` in the manifest's `approximations.reskins`. The creature plays as its SRD self, name included (see "Why SRD-only"); the area prose sets an evocative scene without renaming it. The **reskin is a judgment** (which template is closest); its **result is checkable** (does the emitted `template_id` resolve against stock? — `validate_bundle`). Traps → `TrapSpec`/`TrapEffect`; treasure → `FeatureSpec`/`AreaTreasureSpec`. Bespoke trap logic, puzzles, tricks, NPC roleplay, and any creature no SRD template meaningfully approximates → **flagged for the authorial escape hatch** and logged.

### The deterministic helper tools and the review gates

**Exposure — off the play surface, by design.** These helpers must **not** be registered as `@mcp.tool()` on the play `FastMCP("osrlib-referee")` instance: FastMCP advertises every tool's schema in *every* session that loads the plugin, so a compiler tool on the play server would add a standing per-turn cost to play sessions that never compile anything — the exact standing-schema cost Phase 1 counted line-by-line. Instead the compiler skill invokes them as `uv run` scripts (via Bash) — or, if a live MCP surface is wanted, a **compilation-only** server loaded only when compiling — so they cost play sessions zero.

- **`edge_key(position, direction)`** — a thin wrapper over `osrlib.crawl.dungeon.edge_key`, so the canonical edge keys are computed, never hand-derived.
- **`validate_bundle(bundle)`** — runs `Adventure.model_validate` then `validate_adventure` **passing the stock catalogs** (as `GameSession.new` does, `session.py:273`), so keyed `template_id`s (`adventure.py:142`) and feature `item_id`s (`adventure.py:89`) resolve against stock and every problem is reported at once (`adventure.py:171-172`) — that already catches a reskin that named a non-existent SRD template. On top of that it adds the checks `validate_adventure` genuinely skips: (1) an **edge-key integrity check** — the `edges` map keys are free-form and unvalidated (`dungeon.py:490`), and `.edge()` returns a wall unless **both** cells the edge separates are in bounds (`dungeon.py:522`), so a key naming a boundary edge (owner in bounds, neighbour off-grid — e.g. `"0,0:west"`) is a silent dead entry the engine never reads; `validate_bundle` asserts both cells an edge key separates are `in_bounds` (for `:north`, the owner `(x,y)` and `(x,y-1)`; for `:west`, the owner and `(x-1,y)`) — an in-bounds *grid* test, **not** membership in an `AreaSpec`, since a legitimate corridor edge can belong to a bare cell in no area (Phase 1's own map has such cells, `content.py`); (2) a **wandering-table resolution check** — `validate_adventure` never resolves `WanderingSpec.table` template_ids (only keyed/feature ids), so a reskin used only in a wandering table would pass validation and fail at `spawn`; `validate_bundle` resolves every wandering-table `template_id` against stock too. It also enforces the sharp edges: reserved `'pile'` feature id, feature/area id uniqueness per level, `KeyedMonster` exactly-one-of `count_fixed`/`count_dice`.
- **A map renderer** — renders the compiled grid (cells + edges + doors) back to text for the **visual map-diff review gate**: a human compares the rendered compiled map against the source module's map before the bundle is accepted.

### The honesty note

The **compilation is a one-time, non-reproducible LLM pass** — the same Phase-1 honesty about the `bx-referee` baseline applies. The **bundle it produces is the reproducible artifact**: once compiled and reviewed, a bundle plays deterministically (seed + command log) exactly like native content, and the compile cost amortizes over every future session (versus `bx-referee`'s per-visit PDF re-read). The manifest's `approximations` log makes the non-reproducible step **auditable** — every reskin, every geometry approximation, every escape-hatch beat — so a reviewer sees the fidelity budget explicitly. **Reskin fidelity is the largest fidelity risk this phase carries:** a "barrow wight" reskinned to an SRD `skeleton` *behaves* like a skeleton (different HD, no energy drain) even though the referee narrates it only by appearance; the reviewer decides per creature whether the nearest template is mechanically "close enough" or the beat belongs in the escape hatch instead.

## Work item — the demo module

The DoD needs "one small openly-licensed or original-authored module." Two constraints:

- **Licensing forces open-or-original.** Most published commercial modules cannot be compiled-and-committed (their prose especially). So the demo is either a genuinely small **openly-licensed (OGL/CC) module** — **selection is a scoped task that must verify the license**, not assume it; the OSE SRD in `osrlib/srd/` is OGL monster/item *pages*, not a full keyed adventure — **or** an **original-authored, "published-shaped"** module: a real document with a map, numbered keyed areas, and read-aloud text.
- **The compiler does identical work either way**, and — unlike Phase 1, where content was authored *directly* as Python models — the compiler here reads a *document*. Prefer a verified small OGL/CC module; fall back to an original-authored one, which also sidesteps licensing entirely.
- **Include at least one reskin.** The demo must exercise the reskin path — a named or non-SRD creature reskinned to its nearest SRD template — so the compiler's template-selection judgment, the manifest audit, and `validate_bundle`'s stock-resolution check run on real reskinned content. This is **compile-time** coverage: because a reskinned creature plays byte-identically to a native SRD one, the play-through proves loadability, not anything reskin-specific. A module whose creatures are *all* already SRD would leave the compiler's reskin judgment untested.

## Sequencing

1. **The bundle format + loader + the server changes** — discovery/registry union, session-scoped `prose`, per-adventure save ids — plus `validate_bundle` and the helper tools. Test rungs alongside (the fixture bundle is what everything tests against).
2. **The compiler skill** — prose split, geometry → edges, keyed content **with the reskin stage**, escape-hatch flagging — leaning on the helpers from step 1.
3. **Compile the demo module** — run it through the compiler, pass `validate_bundle` and the visual map-diff review gate, write the approximation log (reskins + geometry + escape-hatch beats).
4. **Milestone — playable:** the manual Claude Code play-through of the compiled bundle succeeds end-to-end through the existing `play` skill (the reskinned creature plays as an ordinary SRD monster — reskin correctness was proven at compile time in step 3).
5. **README/docs** close out.

## Definition of done

- A small **openly-licensed or original-authored** module is **compiled into a bundle and played end-to-end** through the existing tools (`session_new` on the bundle → `observe`/`execute`/`prose` delve → durable save), with `validate_bundle` (edge-key integrity, wandering-table resolution) and the visual map-diff review gate passed, and the approximation/escape-hatch log written — **including every reskin** (`{source_name, template_id, area_id}`). Reskin correctness is a **compile-time** result (the compiler's template-selection judgment, the manifest audit, `validate_bundle`'s stock-resolution check); the play-through re-confirms the bundle loads and plays but adds no reskin-specific coverage, since a reskinned creature is byte-identical in play to a native SRD one.
- The bundle carries **no custom catalog** and requires **no engine change and no behavioral skill change**; every keyed `template_id` resolves against the stock SRD catalog. **Automated proxy done:** the full compile→load→delve passes in-process through the real server tool functions; the manual Claude Code play-through is the final gate (as in Phase 1).
- The test ladder is green in CI: the fixture-bundle round-trip + in-process delve, the `validate_bundle` edge-key and wandering-table checks, the registry-union discovery test, the session-scoped prose test, and the per-adventure save test.
- The demo bundle lives where licensing dictates — in-repo `adventures/` only if openly-licensed/original; otherwise the game directory — with the approximation log documenting what was reskinned, approximated, or pushed to the escape hatch.

## Decisions pinned

- **Phase 2 is scoped to SRD-only content.** The spec's injectable-catalog engine change is dropped and the injection surface deferred; there is **no upstream `osrlib` change** in this phase. The spec was amended (2026-07-06) to match — its roadmap, risks, and pinned/resolved decisions now record the SRD-only Phase 2 and carry the injectable-catalog change forward as a *future* phase gated on a module that genuinely needs it.
- **Non-SRD creatures and items are reskinned to their nearest SRD template — SRD stats and mechanical identity.** The bundle's keyed content carries only stock SRD `template_id`s; a reskinned creature *is* that SRD monster to the engine, and the referee narrates it by appearance (constitution Article II.3), announcing neither the source name nor the SRD name. There is **no prose name-override and no behavioral skill change** — that machinery was considered and rejected as fragile scaffolding for little gain; the engine surfaces the SRD name in player-visible events with no `KeyedMonster` field to override (`events.py:306`, `dungeon.py:394-418`), but constitution Article II.3 already has the referee narrate by appearance, so neither name is spoken either way. Every reskin is logged in `approximations.reskins` as an audit record. A reskinned id resolves exactly like `skeleton`/`giant_rat` today.
- **Genuinely novel stat blocks stay out of scope.** A creature whose mechanics *are* the encounter (no meaningful SRD approximation), and true custom *magic* items, go to the authorial escape hatch or wait for the deferred injectable-catalog change. The approximation log names this boundary per beat.
- **The bundle is a JSON directory** — `adventure.json` (`model_dump`, SRD ids only) + `prose.json` (keyed by `AreaSpec.id`, plus the reserved `"town"` key; scene-setting only) + `manifest.json` (`bundle_id`, name, description, `osrlib_version`, license, `approximations`). **No `catalog.json`.** The prose sidecar is on-disk data, never in-repo Python, because private-module bundles can't be committed.
- **The loader is `Adventure.model_validate` → `GameSession.new`** (self-validates against stock; no catalog passed). Discovery unions native builders with on-disk bundles; `prose` becomes session-scoped; saves disambiguate by `adventure_id` + `save_id`.
- **`validate_bundle` adds two checks `validate_adventure` skips** — edge-key integrity (both cells an edge key separates are in-bounds on the level grid, matching `.edge()`'s own dead-entry rule — *not* area-membership) and wandering-table `template_id` resolution — plus a visual map-diff review gate before acceptance. Keyed/feature id resolution rides `validate_adventure` itself (given stock catalogs).
- **The compiler helper tools stay off the play tool surface** — invoked as `uv run` scripts (or a compilation-only server), never `@mcp.tool()`s on the play server — so they add zero standing per-turn schema cost to play sessions.
- **The compile is a one-time non-reproducible LLM pass; the bundle is the reproducible artifact.** The manifest's approximation log makes the non-reproducible step auditable.
- **Demo module: prefer a verified small OGL/CC module, fall back to original-authored; include ≥1 reskin.** Bundles for non-open modules stay private in the game directory (Licensing).

## Risks and open questions

- **Reskin fidelity is the largest risk.** A reskinned creature *behaves* unlike the source module implies (a "barrow wight" statted as an SRD `skeleton` loses energy drain), even though the referee narrates it only by appearance and announces neither name. This is an accepted trade, not a bug: the approximation log records it, and a reviewer judges per creature whether the nearest SRD template is "close enough" or the beat should go to the escape hatch instead. The compiler surfaces the substitution; it never hides it. Open until the demo's reskins are reviewed at the compile-time map-diff / approximation-log gate.
- **Geometry transcription fidelity** — the dominant compiler effort and the likeliest place fidelity slips. Mitigated by the `edge_key` helper (no hand-derived keys), `validate_bundle`'s edge-key integrity check (catches out-of-bounds dead keys), and the visual map-diff gate. Non-grid maps are an explicit, logged fidelity loss.
- **Demo-module licensing/selection.** Whether a genuinely small OGL/CC keyed module exists that is worth compiling is unverified; the fallback is an original-authored, published-shaped module. Selection must verify the license, not assume it.
- **The deferred boundary is real, not hypothetical.** When a target module's central creature *cannot* be reskinned without gutting the encounter, that module waits for the injectable-catalog engine change. Phase 2 names this honestly rather than pretending SRD-only covers every module; the spec-roadmap update should carry the deferred engine change forward as the answer for that class of content.
